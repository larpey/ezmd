## 1 Overview {#sec-1}

The harbor authority reviewed every berth on the east quay during the spring survey. Inspectors measured the fender wear, logged the bollard loads, and compared the readings with the previous season. Most berths were within tolerance, and the two that were not were scheduled for repair before the summer peak.

This report collects the information the board asked for in January. It covers berth condition, cargo volumes, staffing, and the capital plan for the next two years.

### 1.1 Scope {#sec-1-1}

The survey covered the following areas of the port:

- East quay berths one to six
- The container yard and its lighting
- Rail sidings and the gate complex

Each area was inspected twice, once at low tide and once at high tide.

## 2 Berth condition {#sec-2}

Fender wear was the most common finding. The inspection team recorded information about each fender on a tablet and uploaded it to the asset register the same day.

The harbor authority reviewed every berth on the east quay during the spring survey. Inspectors measured the fender wear, logged the bollard loads, and compared the readings with the previous season. Most berths were within tolerance, and the two that were not were scheduled for repair before the summer peak.

### 2.1 Repairs scheduled {#sec-2-1}

1. Replace fenders on berth three
2. Re-anchor bollard 14
3. Resurface the berth five apron

Repairs are funded from the maintenance reserve and do not need board approval.

## 3 Cargo volumes {#sec-3}

Volumes rose in every quarter except the third, when the rail sidings were closed for two weeks.

[Further detail is published on the port website.](https://example.org/harbor-lane/volumes)

### 3.1 Staffing {#sec-3-1}

Staffing stayed flat at 212 full-time employees. Seasonal hiring added 31 people for the summer peak.

## 4 Capital plan {#sec-4}

The capital plan funds a new gate complex in 2026 and quay lighting in 2027.

The board will review the plan again in the autumn once the tender results are known.
